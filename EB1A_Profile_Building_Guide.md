## How USCIS actually decides (Kazarian two-step)

1. **Step 1 — threshold:** Do at least 3 of the 10 criteria have *documentary* support? This is a checkbox exercise.
2. **Step 2 — final merits:** Looking at *all* evidence together, does it show **sustained national/international acclaim** and that you are **at the very top of your field**? This is where most denials happen — especially since FY2025–FY2026, when approval rates fell from ~75% to ~32% on tougher final-merits review.

**The single most important lesson:** every activity below must produce evidence for *both* steps — a document that checks the box AND objective proof of field-level impact. Confirmation emails alone check the box; they do not win final merits.

## GAP #1 — Criterion #4: Judging the work of others (TOP PRIORITY)

**What USCIS wants.** Step 1: proof you evaluated the work of others in your field (not subordinates, not internal code reviews). Step 2: proof the judging role was *selective and expert-based* — why YOU were chosen, the venue's stature, and that your judgment mattered.

### Activities, ranked by evidentiary weight

1. **Journal peer review (IEEE/ACM)** — heaviest weight. With active IEEE membership and an OpenReview account, you can:
   - Volunteer as reviewer for IEEE Access and IEEE conference submissions (reviewer calls run year-round)
   - Register reviewer interest on OpenReview for ML/systems venues
   - Respond to any Elsevier/IEEE reviewer invitations in your inbox (you have pending reviewer taps — clear them)
   - *Evidence:* official invitation email naming you as reviewer, the journal's impact factor and acceptance rate, editor thank-you/confirmation letters, Publons/Web of Science reviewer record
2. **Conference Technical Program Committee / paper reviewer** — TPC member or reviewer for peer-reviewed IEEE/ACM conferences (e.g., IEEE Big Data Dec 2026 Phoenix, 2027 conferences with open reviewer calls). *Evidence:* TPC appointment letter, conference acceptance rate and attendance stats, program listing with your name
3. **Hackathon judging** — fastest to start; already in motion (AITEX Summit Fall 2026, Boost Hacks II, Opportunity Hack judge applications, Oct 2026). Keep judging 1–2 hackathons/month. *Evidence:* organizer invitation letter stating why you were selected (expertise, not just availability), hackathon scale (participants, sponsors), judging certificate, published judge roster
4. **Award panels** — Globee Awards, Stevie Awards, CODiE Awards all recruit industry judges; a Shamayev-published EB-1 approval used a Globee judging record. *Evidence:* panel appointment letter, award's national/international scope documentation
5. **Pitch competition / thesis evaluation** — venture pitch judging, university capstone/thesis evaluation as external expert. Lower weight but counts.

### Monthly targets (Oct 2026 → Mar 2027)

### Trap

## Common traps — read before every activity

1. **Own papers ≠ #3.** Your publications support #6 only. #3 needs independent coverage *about* you.
2. **Confirmation emails alone don't win step 2.** Every activity needs: invitation/selection letter explaining *why you*, venue stature stats (impact factor, acceptance rate, circulation), and outcome proof (certificate, published roster, editor letter).
3. **No manufactured evidence.** Pay-to-publish journals, purchased awards, paid coverage, and vanity conferences are net negatives.
4. **Judging must be of others' work in the field** — not your team, not your direct reports.
5. **Letters need objective corroboration.** Every claim in a recommendation letter should point to a document (patent, citation, revenue figure, article).
6. **Approval rates are not your odds.** Aggregate quarterly rates (~32% in FY2026 Q3) describe the pool, not your petition. A well-documented case with 6–7 criteria and strong final-merits evidence is a different population.
7. **AAO decisions are persuasive, not binding.** Cite favorable non-precedent decisions (e.g., Matter of O-B-A-, In Re: 35160496) as supporting reasoning, never as guarantees.
8. **Don't delay filing for a PhD.** Your accepted Oct 3 advice stands — file on the evidence plan, not a degree timeline.
